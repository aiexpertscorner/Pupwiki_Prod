# How to Fix Severe Separation Anxiety in Dogs

Separation anxiety occurs when a dog becomes highly distressed upon being separated from their guardian.  It manifests as vocalisation, destruction, escape attempts or even self‑harm and usually occurs only when the guardian is absent【284151969814339†L115-L160】.  Severe cases can be heartbreaking and dangerous, but with a structured plan and patience, you can help your dog cope.

## Symptoms of Separation Anxiety

Common signs include:

* **Urinating or defecating** indoors when alone, despite being house‑trained【284151969814339†L115-L160】.
* **Barking or howling** continuously after departure【284151969814339†L115-L160】.
* **Chewing, digging or destroying** doors, windows or household items【284151969814339†L115-L160】.
* **Escape attempts** through doors or windows that can result in injury【284151969814339†L115-L160】.
* **Pacing and coprophagia** (eating faeces) while the owner is away【284151969814339†L115-L160】.

Triggers include changes in the dog’s family, schedule or environment【284151969814339†L164-L190】.

## Management and Treatment

### 1. Counterconditioning
For mild cases, teach your dog that being alone predicts something wonderful.  Give a stuffed puzzle toy (like a KONG with peanut butter) or a food‑dispensing toy just before leaving; remove it when you return【284151969814339†L250-L270】.  This positive association can reduce anxiety over time.

### 2. Systematic Desensitisation
Severe cases require gradually increasing your dog’s tolerance to being alone.  Start with very short absences (seconds) and slowly extend them.  Use a webcam to monitor your dog.  If they show signs of distress, return before they panic and reduce the duration【284151969814339†L276-L297】.

### 3. Desensitise Predeparture Cues
Dogs often react to cues like picking up keys or putting on shoes.  Repeatedly perform these actions without leaving to break the association【284151969814339†L276-L297】.

### 4. Provide Enrichment and Exercise
Daily mental and physical exercise reduces anxiety and builds confidence.  Scent games, training sessions and interactive toys keep your dog’s mind busy.

### 5. Create a Safe Resting Area
Set up a comfortable space with a cosy bed and white noise.  Provide access to fresh water and consider leaving a shirt that smells like you.

### 6. Seek Professional Help
Complex cases often require a certified behaviourist.  They can tailor a plan and may suggest anti‑anxiety medication in consultation with a veterinarian【281693113410734†L69-L98】.

### 7. Avoid Punishment and Quick Fixes
Punishing anxious behaviour or expecting rapid results can exacerbate the problem【281693113410734†L69-L98】.  Healing takes time; remain patient and consistent.

## Final Words

Separation anxiety is an emotional condition, not disobedience.  With compassionate training, environmental management and sometimes medication, most dogs can learn to feel safe when alone.
