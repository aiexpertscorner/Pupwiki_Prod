### What to prioritise in the first weeks with a Curly-Coated Retriever puppy
Large-breed puppies like the Curly-Coated Retriever need large-breed-specific puppy formulas that control calcium and phosphorus ratios. Avoid generic puppy food — rapid growth from inappropriate nutrition is a major cause of joint problems in large breeds. Limit stairs and jumping until growth plates close at 12–18 months. Schedule a vet visit within the first 72 hours of bringing your Curly-Coated Retriever puppy home. The first-year vet schedule typically includes: 8-week vaccines, 12-week booster, 16-week final puppy booster, 6-month neuter/spay consultation, and 12-month first adult check. The critical socialisation window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds, surfaces, people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A puppy class started at 8–10 weeks provides peer exposure that home training alone cannot replicate.
### Frequently asked questions about Curly-Coated Retriever puppy care
**What does a Curly-Coated Retriever puppy need in the first week?**
In the first week: vet check within 72 hours, a large-breed puppy formula (2–3 meals/day — avoid one large meal), a crate sized for an adult Curly-Coated Retriever, and crate training started with short positive sessions. Limit stairs and jumping — growth plates are open until 12–18 months.
**How do I socialise a Curly-Coated Retriever puppy?**
The critical window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds (traffic, appliances, children), new surfaces (gravel, carpet, grass, pavement), new people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A structured puppy class started at 8–10 weeks is the most efficient route — it covers socialisation and basic obedience in one place. After 16 weeks the window closes — missed socialisation is harder (not impossible) to compensate for later.
**How much does a Curly-Coated Retriever puppy cost in the first year?**
The first year is typically the most expensive — beyond the purchase price, budget for vaccines ($300–$600), supplies/setup ($200–$500), food ($300–$800), and spay/neuter ($200–$600). Use the PupWiki cost calculator for a Curly-Coated Retriever-specific estimate.
