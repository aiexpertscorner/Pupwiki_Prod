## How to choose for a German Longhaired Pointer
Start with AAFCO compliance: the label should state the food is complete and balanced for the appropriate life stage. A clearly named protein (not just "meat" or "poultry") as the first ingredient is the next filter. Large breeds benefit from formulas with controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratios and moderate fat content to support joint health and avoid rapid growth issues. German Longhaired Pointer is an active breed — a higher-protein, moderate-fat formula supports sustained energy without excess weight gain. For a typical adult German Longhaired Pointer (55–80 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 916 kcal/day — with a active-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 1319–1613 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.
### What to look for in food for a German Longhaired Pointer
Large breeds benefit from formulas with a controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratio. Look for products specifically labelled for large or giant breeds, as these manage growth rate and joint load more carefully. German Longhaired Pointers are active dogs — a higher-protein formula (26–30% crude protein) supports muscle maintenance and sustained energy through their exercise demands.
### Frequently asked questions about German Longhaired Pointer feeding
**How much should I feed my German Longhaired Pointer?**
A rough starting point for German Longhaired Pointers (55–80 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a German Longhaired Pointer?**
Look for formulas specifically labelled for large breeds, which manage calcium and phosphorus ratios to support joint development. Avoid puppy formulas designed for small dogs even if your German Longhaired Pointer is young.
**When should I switch my German Longhaired Pointer from puppy to adult food?**
Large breeds should stay on a large-breed puppy formula until 18–24 months, then transition to an adult formula. Switching too early can disrupt joint development during the growth phase.
