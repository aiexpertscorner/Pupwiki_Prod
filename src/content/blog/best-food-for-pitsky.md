## How to approach Pitsky care
Look for AAFCO compliance — the label should confirm the formula is complete and balanced for your dog's life stage. A named protein (chicken, salmon, beef) should be the first ingredient listed. Medium breeds do well on standard adult maintenance formulas — prioritise named protein sources and appropriate calorie density for their activity level. Pitsky is an active breed — a higher-protein, moderate-fat formula supports sustained energy without excess weight gain. High-shedding and double-coated breeds often benefit from omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA) — look for formulas with salmon or fish oil as a listed ingredient. Pitskys are documented for joint conditions (hip dysplasia, allergies, heart issues) — look for formulas listing glucosamine and chondroitin as added ingredients, or plan to add a separate joint supplement after age 5. For a typical adult Pitsky (33.07–77.16 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 781 kcal/day — with a active-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 1125–1375 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.
### What to look for in food for a Pitsky
Medium breeds like the Pitsky do well on standard adult maintenance formulas. Prioritise a named protein source in the first ingredient and an AAFCO adequacy statement for the right life stage. Pitskys are active dogs — a higher-protein formula (26–30% crude protein) supports muscle maintenance and sustained energy through their exercise demands. Pitskys shed seasonally — look for formulas that include omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA from salmon or fish oil) to support coat health from the inside out. Pitskys have a documented risk of joint conditions. Formulas with added glucosamine and chondroitin offer a convenient way to support joint health alongside the diet.
### Frequently asked questions about Pitsky feeding
**How much should I feed my Pitsky?**
A rough starting point for Pitskys (33.07–77.16 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Pitsky?**
An AAFCO-compliant adult maintenance formula with a named protein source (not "meat meal") as the first ingredient is the right baseline. Fresh or freeze-dried toppers can add variety without the commitment of a full diet switch.
**When should I switch my Pitsky from puppy to adult food?**
Most Pitskys can transition to adult food between 12 and 15 months. Transition gradually over 7–10 days to avoid digestive upset.
