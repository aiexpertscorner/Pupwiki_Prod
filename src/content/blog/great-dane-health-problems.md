## About Great Danes
Great Danes are large-sized (110–175 lbs), with active energy, seasonal shedding, and a short coat. The recommendations below are matched to these traits.
### Common health concerns for Great Danes
Great Danes have 4 documented genetic health conditions: heart, spinal, hip problems, fatal stomach bloat. Typical life expectancy is 7–10 years.

### What to know about Great Dane health planning
Great Danes have 4 documented genetic health conditions: **heart, spinal, hip problems, fatal stomach bloat**. Knowing these in advance helps you choose the right insurance timing and ask targeted screening questions at vet appointments. Large breeds like the Great Dane are statistically more prone to joint issues and shorter lifespans. Vet visits every 6 months from age 6 onward are generally recommended over the standard annual check. For any breed with documented genetic conditions, consider enrolling in pet insurance before symptoms appear — most policies exclude pre-existing conditions. With a typical Great Dane life expectancy of 7–10 years, insurance decisions made in the first year can significantly affect lifetime vet coverage.

Great Danes have 4 documented genetic health conditions: heart, spinal, hip problems, fatal stomach bloat. These are breed-level statistical risks — not every Great Dane will develop these conditions. Your vet can recommend targeted screening based on your dog's individual history.
**How long do Great Danes live?**
Great Danes have a typical life expectancy of 7–10 years. Individual lifespan depends on genetics, weight management, preventive care, and whether any genetic conditions develop. Annual vet visits and maintaining a healthy weight are among the most evidence-backed longevity factors for dogs.

### Care tips for Great Dane owners
- Needs 60-90 minutes active exercise daily; thrives with a job or sport.
- Seasonal shedder — daily brushing during spring and fall coat blows is essential.
