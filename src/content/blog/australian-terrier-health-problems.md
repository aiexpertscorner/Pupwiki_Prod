## About Australian Terriers
Australian Terriers are small-sized (15–20 lbs), with active energy, minimal shedding, and a rough coat. The recommendations below are matched to these traits.
### Common health concerns for Australian Terriers
No breed-specific genetic ailments are currently documented for the Australian Terrier (typical life expectancy: 11–15 years). Standard preventive care — annual vet checks, dental hygiene, parasite prevention, and weight management — applies to all breeds. Discuss appropriate screening schedules with your vet.
## How to approach Australian Terrier health
⚠️ Always consult your vet before adding supplements or making changes to your dog's health routine. This page is for comparison and planning only.
### What to know about Australian Terrier health planning
No specific genetic ailments are documented for the Australian Terrier in current breed data. Standard preventive care still applies. Toy and small breeds like the Australian Terrier often live longer but are more prone to dental disease, luxating patellas, and tracheal collapse. Annual dental checks from age 2 onward are more important for small breeds than most owners realise. For any breed with documented genetic conditions, consider enrolling in pet insurance before symptoms appear — most policies exclude pre-existing conditions. With a typical Australian Terrier life expectancy of 11–15 years, insurance decisions made in the first year can significantly affect lifetime vet coverage.
### Frequently asked questions about Australian Terrier health
**What are the most common health problems in Australian Terriers?**
No specific genetic ailments are currently documented for the Australian Terrier. Standard preventive care — annual vet checks, dental hygiene, parasite prevention, and healthy weight — applies to all dogs. Ask your vet whether any breed-specific screening is recommended.
**How long do Australian Terriers live?**
Australian Terriers have a typical life expectancy of 11–15 years. Individual lifespan depends on genetics, weight management, preventive care, and whether any genetic conditions develop. Annual vet visits and maintaining a healthy weight are among the most evidence-backed longevity factors for dogs.
**Should I get pet insurance for a Australian Terrier?**
Pet insurance is worth considering for any dog. Even without breed-specific genetic conditions, unexpected accidents and illnesses affect most dogs over a lifetime. Compare plans by waiting period, reimbursement model, and annual or per-condition limits.
### Care tips for Australian Terrier owners
- Needs 60-90 minutes active exercise daily; thrives with a job or sport.
- Low shedding — regular brushing prevents tangles; minimal shedding management.
