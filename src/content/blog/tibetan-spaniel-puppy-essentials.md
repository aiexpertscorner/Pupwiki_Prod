## About Tibetan Spaniels
With a blunt muzzle and big expressive eyes, a “lion’s mane” around the neck, and a plumed tail elegantly curving over the back, they’re distinctly Tibetan. But are they spaniels? No, not in the Western sense, like Cockers or Cavaliers. Instead, Tibbies recall the ancient traditions that produced Pekes, Pugs, Lhasas, and other unmistakably Asian breeds. Tibbies stand about 10 inches at the shoulder; they move quickly and with purpose. They’re seen in coats of many colors and combinations. The frisky and curious Tibetan Spaniel was bred ages ago for sentinel work on the walls of Tibetan monasteries. Known for a flat, silky coat and “lion’s mane” around the neck, the Tibbie forms a tight, worshipful bond with their humans.

## How to approach a Tibetan Spaniel puppy's first year
Choose puppy-specific formulas, sizes and training tools. Avoid adult-strength supplements and training methods designed for mature dogs.
### What to prioritise in the first weeks with a Tibetan Spaniel puppy
Small-breed puppies like the Tibetan Spaniel have fast metabolisms and small stomachs — feed 3–4 small meals per day until 6 months, then twice daily. Use a small-breed puppy formula; avoid large-breed or all-breed blends with inappropriate calorie density. Schedule a vet visit within the first 72 hours of bringing your Tibetan Spaniel puppy home. The first-year vet schedule typically includes: 8-week vaccines, 12-week booster, 16-week final puppy booster, 6-month neuter/spay consultation, and 12-month first adult check. The critical socialisation window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds, surfaces, people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A puppy class started at 8–10 weeks provides peer exposure that home training alone cannot replicate.
### Frequently asked questions about Tibetan Spaniel puppy care
**What does a Tibetan Spaniel puppy need in the first week?**
In the first week: vet check within 72 hours, a small-breed puppy formula (3–4 small meals/day), a crate the right size for sleeping, and gentle exposure to household sounds. Avoid dog parks and public areas until vaccinations are complete.
**How do I socialise a Tibetan Spaniel puppy?**
The critical window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds (traffic, appliances, children), new surfaces (gravel, carpet, grass, pavement), new people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A structured puppy class started at 8–10 weeks is the most efficient route — it covers socialisation and basic obedience in one place. After 16 weeks the window closes — missed socialisation is harder (not impossible) to compensate for later.
**How much does a Tibetan Spaniel puppy cost in the first year?**
Beyond the purchase price (~$1,050 for a Tibetan Spaniel), the first year typically includes: food (~$396–536 for puppy-stage feeding), puppy vaccines and vet checks ($300–$600), supplies/setup ($200–$500), and spay/neuter ($200–$600). Total first-year cost is often 50–100% higher than subsequent years.
### Care tips for Tibetan Spaniel owners
- Low exercise needs — short daily walks and mental enrichment keep this breed happy.
- Seasonal shedder — daily brushing during spring and fall coat blows is essential.
