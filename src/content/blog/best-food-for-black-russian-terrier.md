## How to approach Black Russian Terrier care
Check for an AAFCO adequacy statement, which confirms the food meets minimum nutritional standards for the correct life stage. A named protein source (chicken, turkey, beef, salmon) should appear as the first ingredient. Large breeds benefit from formulas with controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratios and moderate fat content to support joint health and avoid rapid growth issues. Black Russian Terrier is an active breed — a higher-protein, moderate-fat formula supports sustained energy without excess weight gain. For a typical adult Black Russian Terrier (80–130 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 1269 kcal/day — with a active-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 1827–2233 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.
### What to look for in food for a Black Russian Terrier
Large breeds benefit from formulas with a controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratio. Look for products specifically labelled for large or giant breeds, as these manage growth rate and joint load more carefully. Black Russian Terriers are active dogs — a higher-protein formula (26–30% crude protein) supports muscle maintenance and sustained energy through their exercise demands.
### Frequently asked questions about Black Russian Terrier feeding
**How much should I feed my Black Russian Terrier?**
A rough starting point for Black Russian Terriers (80–130 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Black Russian Terrier?**
Look for formulas specifically labelled for large breeds, which manage calcium and phosphorus ratios to support joint development. Avoid puppy formulas designed for small dogs even if your Black Russian Terrier is young.
**When should I switch my Black Russian Terrier from puppy to adult food?**
Large breeds should stay on a large-breed puppy formula until 18–24 months, then transition to an adult formula. Switching too early can disrupt joint development during the growth phase.
