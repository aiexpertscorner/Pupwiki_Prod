## How to approach Frug care
Start with AAFCO compliance: the label should state the food is complete and balanced for the appropriate life stage. A clearly named protein (not just "meat" or "poultry") as the first ingredient is the next filter. Medium breeds do well on standard adult maintenance formulas — prioritise named protein sources and appropriate calorie density for their activity level. Frug has a lower activity level — choose a formula with controlled fat content to avoid obesity, which is a common risk for lower-energy dogs. High-shedding and double-coated breeds often benefit from omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA) — look for formulas with salmon or fish oil as a listed ingredient. For a typical adult Frug (17.64–30.86 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 420 kcal/day — with a calm-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 454–554 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone. PupWiki care note: Watch heat and exercise tolerance closely, especially in warm weather or high humidity.
### What to look for in food for a Frug
Medium breeds like the Frug do well on standard adult maintenance formulas. Prioritise a named protein source in the first ingredient and an AAFCO adequacy statement for the right life stage. Frugs tend toward lower activity — choose a formula with controlled fat content to avoid the weight gain that commonly affects lower-energy breeds over time. Frugs shed seasonally — look for formulas that include omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA from salmon or fish oil) to support coat health from the inside out.
### Frequently asked questions about Frug feeding
**How much should I feed my Frug?**
A rough starting point for Frugs (17.64–30.86 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Frug?**
An AAFCO-compliant adult maintenance formula with a named protein source (not "meat meal") as the first ingredient is the right baseline. Fresh or freeze-dried toppers can add variety without the commitment of a full diet switch.
**When should I switch my Frug from puppy to adult food?**
Most Frugs can transition to adult food between 12 and 15 months. Transition gradually over 7–10 days to avoid digestive upset.
