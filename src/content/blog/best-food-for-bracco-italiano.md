## How to choose for a Bracco Italiano
Prioritise formulas with an AAFCO adequacy statement — this confirms the recipe is complete and balanced. Named proteins should appear before grains in the ingredient list. Large breeds benefit from formulas with controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratios and moderate fat content to support joint health and avoid rapid growth issues. Bracco Italiano is an active breed — a higher-protein, moderate-fat formula supports sustained energy without excess weight gain. For a typical adult Bracco Italiano (55–90 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 966 kcal/day — with a active-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 1391–1701 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.
### What to look for in food for a Bracco Italiano
Large breeds benefit from formulas with a controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratio. Look for products specifically labelled for large or giant breeds, as these manage growth rate and joint load more carefully. Bracco Italianos are active dogs — a higher-protein formula (26–30% crude protein) supports muscle maintenance and sustained energy through their exercise demands.
### Frequently asked questions about Bracco Italiano feeding
**How much should I feed my Bracco Italiano?**
A rough starting point for Bracco Italianos (55–90 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Bracco Italiano?**
Look for formulas specifically labelled for large breeds, which manage calcium and phosphorus ratios to support joint development. Avoid puppy formulas designed for small dogs even if your Bracco Italiano is young.
**When should I switch my Bracco Italiano from puppy to adult food?**
Large breeds should stay on a large-breed puppy formula until 18–24 months, then transition to an adult formula. Switching too early can disrupt joint development during the growth phase.
