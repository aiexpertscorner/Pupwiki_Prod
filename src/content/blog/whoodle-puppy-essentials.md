## About Whoodles
Whoodle is a designer cross between the Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier and the Poodle (Standard). This mix usually weighs about 33-44 pounds, often has a curly, low-shed-leaning coat, and often blends an active, people-oriented temperament from both sides of the pedigree. ## How to approach a Whoodle puppy's first year
Choose puppy-specific formulas, sizes and training tools. Avoid adult-strength supplements and training methods designed for mature dogs.
### What to prioritise in the first weeks with a Whoodle puppy
Medium-breed puppies do well on standard puppy formulas — look for AAFCO "growth" or "all life stages" labelling. Transition to adult food between 12 and 15 months. Schedule a vet visit within the first 72 hours of bringing your Whoodle puppy home. The first-year vet schedule typically includes: 8-week vaccines, 12-week booster, 16-week final puppy booster, 6-month neuter/spay consultation, and 12-month first adult check. The critical socialisation window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds, surfaces, people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A puppy class started at 8–10 weeks provides peer exposure that home training alone cannot replicate.
### Frequently asked questions about Whoodle puppy care
**What does a Whoodle puppy need in the first week?**
In the first week: vet check within 72 hours, a puppy formula appropriate for medium breeds (3 meals/day until 6 months), crate introduction with positive reinforcement, and household exposure. Avoid public dog areas until vaccines are complete.
**How do I socialise a Whoodle puppy?**
The critical window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds (traffic, appliances, children), new surfaces (gravel, carpet, grass, pavement), new people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A structured puppy class started at 8–10 weeks is the most efficient route — it covers socialisation and basic obedience in one place. After 16 weeks the window closes — missed socialisation is harder (not impossible) to compensate for later.
**How much does a Whoodle puppy cost in the first year?**
Beyond the purchase price (~$1,340 for a Whoodle), the first year typically includes: food (~$332–448 for puppy-stage feeding), puppy vaccines and vet checks ($300–$600), supplies/setup ($200–$500), and spay/neuter ($200–$600). Total first-year cost is often 50–100% higher than subsequent years.
### Care tips for Whoodle owners
- Needs 60-90 minutes of purposeful daily exercise plus mental work to stay balanced.
- Coat can mat quickly—brush several times per week and plan regular professional grooming.
- Consistency matters—keep sessions short, reward-based, and started early for best results.
