## How to choose for a Caucasian Shepherd Dog
Start with AAFCO compliance: the label should state the food is complete and balanced for the appropriate life stage. A clearly named protein (not just "meat" or "poultry") as the first ingredient is the next filter. Large breeds benefit from formulas with controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratios and moderate fat content to support joint health and avoid rapid growth issues. Caucasian Shepherd Dog has a lower activity level — choose a formula with controlled fat content to avoid obesity, which is a common risk for lower-energy dogs. High-shedding and double-coated breeds often benefit from omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA) — look for formulas with salmon or fish oil as a listed ingredient. For a typical adult Caucasian Shepherd Dog (99–170 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 1532 kcal/day — with a calm-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 1654–2022 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.
### What to look for in food for a Caucasian Shepherd Dog
Large breeds benefit from formulas with a controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratio. Look for products specifically labelled for large or giant breeds, as these manage growth rate and joint load more carefully. Caucasian Shepherd Dogs tend toward lower activity — choose a formula with controlled fat content to avoid the weight gain that commonly affects lower-energy breeds over time. Caucasian Shepherd Dogs shed seasonally — look for formulas that include omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA from salmon or fish oil) to support coat health from the inside out.
### Frequently asked questions about Caucasian Shepherd Dog feeding
**How much should I feed my Caucasian Shepherd Dog?**
A rough starting point for Caucasian Shepherd Dogs (99–170 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Caucasian Shepherd Dog?**
Look for formulas specifically labelled for large breeds, which manage calcium and phosphorus ratios to support joint development. Avoid puppy formulas designed for small dogs even if your Caucasian Shepherd Dog is young.
**When should I switch my Caucasian Shepherd Dog from puppy to adult food?**
Large breeds should stay on a large-breed puppy formula until 18–24 months, then transition to an adult formula. Switching too early can disrupt joint development during the growth phase.
