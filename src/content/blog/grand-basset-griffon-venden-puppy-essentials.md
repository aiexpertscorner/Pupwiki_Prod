## About Grand Basset Griffon Vendéens
First, let’s deal with the name. Roughly translated, it means “Large, low, shaggy dog of the Vendée” and is pronounced “Grand-Bah-SAY Gree-FOHN VON-day-uhn.” Now, let’s have a look at the dog: He’s a sweet-faced, long-eared fellow in a shaggy coat whose mustache, beard, and profuse eyebrows suggest the look of a worldly but amiable Frenchman. Beneath the Grand’s Old World charm is a rugged, sturdily-built bruiser who is deceptively quick and light-footed. The stamina and courage of these longer-than-tall hunters is the stuff of Gallic legend. The Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen is a French scenthound. Somewhat active and never high-strung, yet GBGVs are busy dogs who don’t tire easily.

## How to approach a Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen puppy's first year
Choose puppy-specific formulas, sizes and training tools. Avoid adult-strength supplements and training methods designed for mature dogs.
### What to prioritise in the first weeks with a Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen puppy
Medium-breed puppies do well on standard puppy formulas — look for AAFCO "growth" or "all life stages" labelling. Transition to adult food between 12 and 15 months. Schedule a vet visit within the first 72 hours of bringing your Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen puppy home. The first-year vet schedule typically includes: 8-week vaccines, 12-week booster, 16-week final puppy booster, 6-month neuter/spay consultation, and 12-month first adult check. The critical socialisation window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds, surfaces, people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A puppy class started at 8–10 weeks provides peer exposure that home training alone cannot replicate.
### Frequently asked questions about Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen puppy care
**What does a Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen puppy need in the first week?**
In the first week: vet check within 72 hours, a puppy formula appropriate for medium breeds (3 meals/day until 6 months), crate introduction with positive reinforcement, and household exposure. Avoid public dog areas until vaccines are complete.
**How do I socialise a Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen puppy?**
The critical window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds (traffic, appliances, children), new surfaces (gravel, carpet, grass, pavement), new people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A structured puppy class started at 8–10 weeks is the most efficient route — it covers socialisation and basic obedience in one place. After 16 weeks the window closes — missed socialisation is harder (not impossible) to compensate for later.
**How much does a Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen puppy cost in the first year?**
The first year is typically the most expensive — beyond the purchase price, budget for vaccines ($300–$600), supplies/setup ($200–$500), food ($300–$800), and spay/neuter ($200–$600). Use the PupWiki cost calculator for a Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen-specific estimate.
### Care tips for Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen owners
- Requires 90+ minutes vigorous exercise daily — fetch, agility, or long hikes.
- Seasonal shedder — daily brushing during spring and fall coat blows is essential.
