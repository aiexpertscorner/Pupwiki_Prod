### What to know about Scottish Deerhound health planning
No specific genetic ailments are documented for the Scottish Deerhound in current breed data. Standard preventive care still applies. Large breeds like the Scottish Deerhound are statistically more prone to joint issues and shorter lifespans. Vet visits every 6 months from age 6 onward are generally recommended over the standard annual check. For any breed with documented genetic conditions, consider enrolling in pet insurance before symptoms appear — most policies exclude pre-existing conditions. With a typical Scottish Deerhound life expectancy of 8–11 years, insurance decisions made in the first year can significantly affect lifetime vet coverage.
