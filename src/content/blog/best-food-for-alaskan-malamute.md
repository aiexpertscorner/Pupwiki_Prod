## How to approach Alaskan Malamute care
Prioritise formulas with an AAFCO adequacy statement — this confirms the recipe is complete and balanced. Named proteins should appear before grains in the ingredient list. Large breeds benefit from formulas with controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratios and moderate fat content to support joint health and avoid rapid growth issues. Alaskan Malamute is an active breed — a higher-protein, moderate-fat formula supports sustained energy without excess weight gain. High-shedding and double-coated breeds often benefit from omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA) — look for formulas with salmon or fish oil as a listed ingredient. Alaskan Malamutes are documented for joint conditions (hip problems, dwarfism) — look for formulas listing glucosamine and chondroitin as added ingredients, or plan to add a separate joint supplement after age 5. For a typical adult Alaskan Malamute (75–85 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 1035 kcal/day — with a active-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 1490–1822 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.

Alaskan Malamutes have a documented risk of joint conditions. Formulas with added glucosamine and chondroitin offer a convenient way to support joint health alongside the diet.
### Frequently asked questions about Alaskan Malamute feeding
**How much should I feed my Alaskan Malamute?**
A rough starting point for Alaskan Malamutes (75–85 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Alaskan Malamute?**
Look for formulas specifically labelled for large breeds, which manage calcium and phosphorus ratios to support joint development. Avoid puppy formulas designed for small dogs even if your Alaskan Malamute is young.
**When should I switch my Alaskan Malamute from puppy to adult food?**
Large breeds should stay on a large-breed puppy formula until 18–24 months, then transition to an adult formula. Switching too early can disrupt joint development during the growth phase.
