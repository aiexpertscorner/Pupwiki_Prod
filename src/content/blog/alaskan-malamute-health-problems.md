## About Alaskan Malamutes
Alaskan Malamutes are large-sized (75–85 lbs), with active energy, seasonal shedding, and a double coat. The recommendations below are matched to these traits.
### Common health concerns for Alaskan Malamutes
Alaskan Malamutes have 2 documented genetic health conditions: hip problems, dwarfism. Typical life expectancy is 10–14 years.
- **Hip problems**: Ask your vet about recommended screening and prevention for this condition in Alaskan Malamutes.
- **Dwarfism**: Ask your vet about recommended screening and prevention for this condition in Alaskan Malamutes.

## How to approach Alaskan Malamute health

### What to know about Alaskan Malamute health planning
Alaskan Malamutes have 2 documented genetic health conditions: **hip problems, dwarfism**. Knowing these in advance helps you choose the right insurance timing and ask targeted screening questions at vet appointments. Large breeds like the Alaskan Malamute are statistically more prone to joint issues and shorter lifespans. Vet visits every 6 months from age 6 onward are generally recommended over the standard annual check. For any breed with documented genetic conditions, consider enrolling in pet insurance before symptoms appear — most policies exclude pre-existing conditions. With a typical Alaskan Malamute life expectancy of 10–14 years, insurance decisions made in the first year can significantly affect lifetime vet coverage.
### Frequently asked questions about Alaskan Malamute health
**What are the most common health problems in Alaskan Malamutes?**
Alaskan Malamutes have 2 documented genetic health conditions: hip problems, dwarfism. These are breed-level statistical risks — not every Alaskan Malamute will develop these conditions. Your vet can recommend targeted screening based on your dog's individual history.
**How long do Alaskan Malamutes live?**
Alaskan Malamutes have a typical life expectancy of 10–14 years. Individual lifespan depends on genetics, weight management, preventive care, and whether any genetic conditions develop. Annual vet visits and maintaining a healthy weight are among the most evidence-backed longevity factors for dogs.
**Should I get pet insurance for a Alaskan Malamute?**
With 2 documented genetic conditions, pet insurance can help manage financial risk for Alaskan Malamutes. Get a quote before your puppy's first vet visit — some conditions can be flagged as pre-existing quickly.
### Care tips for Alaskan Malamute owners
- Needs 60-90 minutes active exercise daily; thrives with a job or sport.
- Seasonal shedder — daily brushing during spring and fall coat blows is essential.
- Brush 2-3× weekly to maintain coat condition and reduce shedding around the home.
