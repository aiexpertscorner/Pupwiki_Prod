### What to know about Boxer health planning
Boxers have 4 documented genetic health conditions: **eye, nerve, heart problems**. Knowing these in advance helps you choose the right insurance timing and ask targeted screening questions at vet appointments. Large breeds like the Boxer are statistically more prone to joint issues and shorter lifespans. Vet visits every 6 months from age 6 onward are generally recommended over the standard annual check. For any breed with documented genetic conditions, consider enrolling in pet insurance before symptoms appear — most policies exclude pre-existing conditions. With a typical Boxer life expectancy of 10–12 years, insurance decisions made in the first year can significantly affect lifetime vet coverage.
### Frequently asked questions about Boxer health
**What are the most common health problems in Boxers?**
Boxers have 4 documented genetic health conditions: eye, nerve, heart problems. These are breed-level statistical risks — not every Boxer will develop these conditions. Your vet can recommend targeted screening based on your dog's individual history.
**How long do Boxers live?**
Boxers have a typical life expectancy of 10–12 years. Individual lifespan depends on genetics, weight management, preventive care, and whether any genetic conditions develop. Annual vet visits and maintaining a healthy weight are among the most evidence-backed longevity factors for dogs.
