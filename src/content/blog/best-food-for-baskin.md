## How to approach Baskin care
Check for an AAFCO adequacy statement, which confirms the food meets minimum nutritional standards for the correct life stage. A named protein source (chicken, turkey, beef, salmon) should appear as the first ingredient. Medium breeds do well on standard adult maintenance formulas — prioritise named protein sources and appropriate calorie density for their activity level. Baskin has a lower activity level — choose a formula with controlled fat content to avoid obesity, which is a common risk for lower-energy dogs. For a typical adult Baskin (28.66–39.68 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 545 kcal/day — with a calm-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 589–719 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.

### Frequently asked questions about Baskin feeding
**How much should I feed my Baskin?**
A rough starting point for Baskins (28.66–39.68 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Baskin?**
An AAFCO-compliant adult maintenance formula with a named protein source (not "meat meal") as the first ingredient is the right baseline. Fresh or freeze-dried toppers can add variety without the commitment of a full diet switch.
**When should I switch my Baskin from puppy to adult food?**
Most Baskins can transition to adult food between 12 and 15 months. Transition gradually over 7–10 days to avoid digestive upset.
