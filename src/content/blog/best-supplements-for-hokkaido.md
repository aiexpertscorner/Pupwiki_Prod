## About Hokkaidos
The Hokkaido is a medium-sized, strongly-built dog. They have longer, thicker coats than the other Japanese breeds, and also have wider chests and smaller ears. Like all the Nihon Ken, they have a double coat made up of protective, coarse outer guard hairs, and a fine, thick undercoat that is shed seasonally. The breed comes in several colors: white, red, black, brindle, sesame, and wolf grey. With early training, the Hokkaido is a very loyal and dedicated companion who wants to please his human family. They are incredibly intelligent thinkers and problem solvers, and they excel at tasks given to them. If not socialized properly, however, Hokkaido can become wary of strangers and protective of their families. The breed is extremely rare outside its native country. ### What to look for in supplements for a Hokkaido
⚠️ Always discuss supplements with your vet before starting. Supplements are not regulated as rigorously as medications — quality and dosing vary widely between products. No breed-specific supplement priority is indicated by current genetic data for the Hokkaido. A balanced AAFCO-compliant diet is the baseline — supplements fill specific gaps identified by your vet, not general prevention. When evaluating supplements, prioritise products with NASC (National Animal Supplement Council) certification, a published Certificate of Analysis (CoA) for third-party testing, and a clearly stated active-ingredient concentration on the label.
### Frequently asked questions about Hokkaido supplements
**What supplements are most relevant for a Hokkaido?**
No breed-specific supplement priority is clearly indicated for the Hokkaido. Omega-3 fatty acids are broadly supported for coat health and inflammation. Joint supplements become relevant around age 5+ for many breeds. Always confirm with your vet before starting any supplement.
**Are supplements safe for Hokkaidos?**
Dog supplements are not FDA-regulated the same way medications are — quality varies significantly between brands. Choose products with NASC certification, a published Certificate of Analysis (CoA) from third-party testing, and clearly stated active ingredient concentrations. Always disclose supplement use to your vet, as interactions with medications and health conditions are possible.
**At what age should I start supplements for a Hokkaido?**
For large breeds like the Hokkaido, joint supplements can be introduced as early as age 3–5 — before symptoms develop — if your vet agrees. This is particularly relevant for breeds with documented joint conditions. Don't wait until limping or stiffness appears if genetic risk is high.
