## About Lancashire Heelers
Lancashire Heelers are small-sized (9–17 lbs), with active energy, seasonal shedding, and a double coat. The recommendations below are matched to these traits.
## How to approach a Lancashire Heeler puppy's first year
Choose puppy-specific formulas, sizes and training tools. Avoid adult-strength supplements and training methods designed for mature dogs.
### What to prioritise in the first weeks with a Lancashire Heeler puppy
Small-breed puppies like the Lancashire Heeler have fast metabolisms and small stomachs — feed 3–4 small meals per day until 6 months, then twice daily. Use a small-breed puppy formula; avoid large-breed or all-breed blends with inappropriate calorie density. Schedule a vet visit within the first 72 hours of bringing your Lancashire Heeler puppy home. The first-year vet schedule typically includes: 8-week vaccines, 12-week booster, 16-week final puppy booster, 6-month neuter/spay consultation, and 12-month first adult check. The critical socialisation window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds, surfaces, people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A puppy class started at 8–10 weeks provides peer exposure that home training alone cannot replicate.
### Frequently asked questions about Lancashire Heeler puppy care
**What does a Lancashire Heeler puppy need in the first week?**
In the first week: vet check within 72 hours, a small-breed puppy formula (3–4 small meals/day), a crate the right size for sleeping, and gentle exposure to household sounds. Avoid dog parks and public areas until vaccinations are complete.
**How do I socialise a Lancashire Heeler puppy?**
The critical window is 8–16 weeks. Each week, introduce new sounds (traffic, appliances, children), new surfaces (gravel, carpet, grass, pavement), new people, and vaccinated calm dogs. A structured puppy class started at 8–10 weeks is the most efficient route — it covers socialisation and basic obedience in one place. After 16 weeks the window closes — missed socialisation is harder (not impossible) to compensate for later.
**How much does a Lancashire Heeler puppy cost in the first year?**
The first year is typically the most expensive — beyond the purchase price, budget for vaccines ($300–$600), supplies/setup ($200–$500), food ($300–$800), and spay/neuter ($200–$600). Use the PupWiki cost calculator for a Lancashire Heeler-specific estimate.
### Care tips for Lancashire Heeler owners
- Needs 60-90 minutes active exercise daily; thrives with a job or sport.
- Seasonal shedder — daily brushing during spring and fall coat blows is essential.
