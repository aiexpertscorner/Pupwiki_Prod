## How to approach Bernedoodle care
An AAFCO-approved formula with a whole protein source — chicken, beef, salmon, or turkey — listed first is the baseline standard. Avoid formulas where the first ingredient is a grain or generic "meat by-products". Large breeds benefit from formulas with controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratios and moderate fat content to support joint health and avoid rapid growth issues. Bernedoodle is an active breed — a higher-protein, moderate-fat formula supports sustained energy without excess weight gain. Bernedoodles are documented for joint conditions (hip dysplasia, elbow issues, certain cancers) — look for formulas listing glucosamine and chondroitin as added ingredients, or plan to add a separate joint supplement after age 5. For a typical adult Bernedoodle (55.12–99.21 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 1006 kcal/day — with a active-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 1449–1771 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.

Bernedoodles have a documented risk of joint conditions. Formulas with added glucosamine and chondroitin offer a convenient way to support joint health alongside the diet.
### Frequently asked questions about Bernedoodle feeding
**How much should I feed my Bernedoodle?**
A rough starting point for Bernedoodles (55.12–99.21 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Bernedoodle?**
Look for formulas specifically labelled for large breeds, which manage calcium and phosphorus ratios to support joint development. Avoid puppy formulas designed for small dogs even if your Bernedoodle is young.
**When should I switch my Bernedoodle from puppy to adult food?**
Large breeds should stay on a large-breed puppy formula until 18–24 months, then transition to an adult formula. Switching too early can disrupt joint development during the growth phase.
