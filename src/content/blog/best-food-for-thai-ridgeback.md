## How to approach Thai Ridgeback care
Start with AAFCO compliance: the label should state the food is complete and balanced for the appropriate life stage. A clearly named protein (not just "meat" or "poultry") as the first ingredient is the next filter. Large breeds benefit from formulas with controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratios and moderate fat content to support joint health and avoid rapid growth issues. Thai Ridgeback is an active breed — a higher-protein, moderate-fat formula supports sustained energy without excess weight gain. For a typical adult Thai Ridgeback (35–75 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 781 kcal/day — with a active-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 1125–1375 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.
### What to look for in food for a Thai Ridgeback
Large breeds benefit from formulas with a controlled calcium-to-phosphorus ratio. Look for products specifically labelled for large or giant breeds, as these manage growth rate and joint load more carefully. Thai Ridgebacks are active dogs — a higher-protein formula (26–30% crude protein) supports muscle maintenance and sustained energy through their exercise demands.
### Frequently asked questions about Thai Ridgeback feeding
**How much should I feed my Thai Ridgeback?**
A rough starting point for Thai Ridgebacks (35–75 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Thai Ridgeback?**
Look for formulas specifically labelled for large breeds, which manage calcium and phosphorus ratios to support joint development. Avoid puppy formulas designed for small dogs even if your Thai Ridgeback is young.
**When should I switch my Thai Ridgeback from puppy to adult food?**
Large breeds should stay on a large-breed puppy formula until 18–24 months, then transition to an adult formula. Switching too early can disrupt joint development during the growth phase.
