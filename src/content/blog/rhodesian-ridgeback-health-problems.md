## About Rhodesian Ridgebacks
Beneath the Ridgeback’s trademark ridge is a whole lot of hound: Ridgebacks are fast and powerful athletes who can weigh between 70 and 85 pounds, and oftentimes more. They come in only one color – wheaten – which spans every shade seen in a wheat field, from pale flaxen to the burnished red of a maturing crop. Ridgebacks also have two nose colors: black and the less commonly seen brown.The formidable Ridgeback can be strong willed, independent, and sometimes domineering. Ridgebacks must be guided with a firm but fair hand from puppyhood. They are faithful friends, protective of their loved ones and meltingly affectionate with those whom they trust. Still, a Ridgeback can be too much hound for the novice dog owner. ### What to know about Rhodesian Ridgeback health planning
Rhodesian Ridgebacks have 2 documented genetic health conditions: **birth defects, hip problems**. Knowing these in advance helps you choose the right insurance timing and ask targeted screening questions at vet appointments. Large breeds like the Rhodesian Ridgeback are statistically more prone to joint issues and shorter lifespans. Vet visits every 6 months from age 6 onward are generally recommended over the standard annual check. For any breed with documented genetic conditions, consider enrolling in pet insurance before symptoms appear — most policies exclude pre-existing conditions. With a typical Rhodesian Ridgeback life expectancy of 10 years, insurance decisions made in the first year can significantly affect lifetime vet coverage.
### Frequently asked questions about Rhodesian Ridgeback health
**What are the most common health problems in Rhodesian Ridgebacks?**
Rhodesian Ridgebacks have 2 documented genetic health conditions: birth defects, hip problems. These are breed-level statistical risks — not every Rhodesian Ridgeback will develop these conditions. Your vet can recommend targeted screening based on your dog's individual history.
**How long do Rhodesian Ridgebacks live?**
Rhodesian Ridgebacks have a typical life expectancy of 10 years. Individual lifespan depends on genetics, weight management, preventive care, and whether any genetic conditions develop. Annual vet visits and maintaining a healthy weight are among the most evidence-backed longevity factors for dogs.
**Should I get pet insurance for a Rhodesian Ridgeback?**
With 2 documented genetic conditions, pet insurance can help manage financial risk for Rhodesian Ridgebacks. Get a quote before your puppy's first vet visit — some conditions can be flagged as pre-existing quickly.
