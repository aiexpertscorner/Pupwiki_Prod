## How to approach Borador care
Start with AAFCO compliance: the label should state the food is complete and balanced for the appropriate life stage. A clearly named protein (not just "meat" or "poultry") as the first ingredient is the next filter. Medium breeds do well on standard adult maintenance formulas — prioritise named protein sources and appropriate calorie density for their activity level. Borador is an active breed — a higher-protein, moderate-fat formula supports sustained energy without excess weight gain. High-shedding and double-coated breeds often benefit from omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA) — look for formulas with salmon or fish oil as a listed ingredient. Boradors are documented for joint conditions (collie eye anomaly (cea), hip dysplasia) — look for formulas listing glucosamine and chondroitin as added ingredients, or plan to add a separate joint supplement after age 5. For a typical adult Borador (39.68–61.73 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 738 kcal/day — with a active-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 1063–1299 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.
### What to look for in food for a Borador
Medium breeds like the Borador do well on standard adult maintenance formulas. Prioritise a named protein source in the first ingredient and an AAFCO adequacy statement for the right life stage. Boradors are active dogs — a higher-protein formula (26–30% crude protein) supports muscle maintenance and sustained energy through their exercise demands. Boradors shed seasonally — look for formulas that include omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA from salmon or fish oil) to support coat health from the inside out. Boradors have a documented risk of joint conditions. Formulas with added glucosamine and chondroitin offer a convenient way to support joint health alongside the diet.
### Frequently asked questions about Borador feeding
**How much should I feed my Borador?**
A rough starting point for Boradors (39.68–61.73 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a Borador?**
An AAFCO-compliant adult maintenance formula with a named protein source (not "meat meal") as the first ingredient is the right baseline. Fresh or freeze-dried toppers can add variety without the commitment of a full diet switch.
**When should I switch my Borador from puppy to adult food?**
Most Boradors can transition to adult food between 12 and 15 months. Transition gradually over 7–10 days to avoid digestive upset.
