## How to approach English Boodle care
An AAFCO-approved formula with a whole protein source — chicken, beef, salmon, or turkey — listed first is the baseline standard. Avoid formulas where the first ingredient is a grain or generic "meat by-products". Medium breeds do well on standard adult maintenance formulas — prioritise named protein sources and appropriate calorie density for their activity level. English Boodles are documented for joint conditions (skin fold dermatitis, hip dysplasia) — look for formulas listing glucosamine and chondroitin as added ingredients, or plan to add a separate joint supplement after age 5. For a typical adult English Boodle (39.68–61.73 lbs), the estimated resting energy requirement is around 738 kcal/day — with a regular-energy activity factor, daily intake sits roughly in the 930–1136 kcal range. Most quality kibbles list kcal/cup on the bag; use this to portion accurately rather than relying on the default "suggested feeding" table alone.
### What to look for in food for a English Boodle
Medium breeds like the English Boodle do well on standard adult maintenance formulas. Prioritise a named protein source in the first ingredient and an AAFCO adequacy statement for the right life stage. Match calorie density to your individual dog's actual activity level, not just the breed average — a less active English Boodle needs fewer calories than an athletic one. English Boodles have a documented risk of joint conditions. Formulas with added glucosamine and chondroitin offer a convenient way to support joint health alongside the diet.
### Frequently asked questions about English Boodle feeding
**How much should I feed my English Boodle?**
A rough starting point for English Boodles (39.68–61.73 lbs) is 1–2 cups per day for smaller adults and 3–4 cups for larger ones — always follow the feeding guidelines on the specific formula and adjust based on body condition, not just weight.
**What food formula works best for a English Boodle?**
An AAFCO-compliant adult maintenance formula with a named protein source (not "meat meal") as the first ingredient is the right baseline. Fresh or freeze-dried toppers can add variety without the commitment of a full diet switch.
**When should I switch my English Boodle from puppy to adult food?**
Most English Boodles can transition to adult food between 12 and 15 months. Transition gradually over 7–10 days to avoid digestive upset.
